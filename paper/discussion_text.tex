\paragraph{What the results support.} On synthetic clouds drawn from the 13-family generative model, a network trained only on synthetic data identifies the family and recovers the shape better than a classical pipeline that fits every family and selects with geometric AIC (H1, H2), and its probabilities are well calibrated after temperature scaling. This is a \emph{systematic} claim within the synthetic distribution (\nMethod{} test clouds over all point-count and noise bins), with paired bootstrap CIs. The ablation A5 localises the advantage to model selection: given the same refined candidates, geometric AIC selects much worse than the learned classifier.

\paragraph{What the results do not support.} The fitter is not yet a reliable replacement for by-eye shape calls on real activations. Coarse agreement with the literature (H3) is not above a permutation null, and the failures have identifiable causes: curves outside the vocabulary (GPT-2 positions), more than six discrete groups (weekdays, months), clusters of unequal spread (true/false statements), branching curves (age), and top-3 projections of higher-dimensional structure (years, grokked embeddings). In each case the classifier still returns a confident label, because a closed-set classifier must pick one of its 13 families. Smooth ordinal controls are frequently labelled helix (\hfourGpHelix{} of the Gaussian-process controls, post hoc), so a helix label from the classifier alone is not evidence of a helix; the preregistered two-part rule (probability ratio and held-out residual) is necessary and rejected the helix claim where it was evaluated.

\paragraph{Implications for practice.} (1) Report the shape of the top-3 projection together with the explained variance and the $\lambda_3/\lambda_4$ gap; a low gap means the 3D picture is a slice. (2) Treat ``helix'' as a hypothesis to be tested against the PCA ringing artifact \citep{novembre2008horseshoe,antognini2018walks,shinn2023phantom} with held-out data, not as a reading of the plot. (3) Controls with random weights are not structureless when inputs differ in token identity; a token-identity-matched control is the right null for prompt-based datasets. (4) Exclude sequence position 0 (BOS or the first token) when averaging activations: attention-sink tokens carry massive activations \citep{sun2024massive,xiao2024sinks} that can dominate class means.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{One seed, no trained ablations yet.} All results come from one trained model (\texttt{main\_s0}); the seed, learning-rate and inference-hyperparameter sweeps and the trained ablations A1, A2, A4, A6 (including H5a) are implemented but postponed to later stages (\texttt{reports/deviations.md}). Synthetic CIs therefore reflect test-sample variation only, not training variation.
\item \textbf{Baselines on a subset.} B1 and B2 were run on 20 clouds per family and cell (\nBone{} clouds) because B1 refines 61 candidate fits per cloud; all paired comparisons use this subset.
\item \textbf{Closed vocabulary.} 13 families with label margins between nested families; shapes outside the vocabulary are forced into one of them. There is no abstention beyond the OOD and gap flags.
\item \textbf{Synthetic prior.} Training data follow hand-designed families and nuisances; real activation clouds differ (non-uniform density along curves, token-level clusters, outliers), which is the main source of the synthetic-to-real gap.
\item \textbf{Top-3 PCA only.} All statements concern the top-3 projection, as the task specifies; intrinsic manifolds can differ, and causal roles of these representations are not tested.
\item \textbf{Literature labels as ground truth.} H3 compares against published descriptions that were themselves made by eye, often with different models, layers or prompts (we use Llama-3.1-8B for all LLM datasets).
\end{itemize}
