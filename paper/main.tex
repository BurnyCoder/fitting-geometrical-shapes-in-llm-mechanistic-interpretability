\begin{abstract}
Interpretability research repeatedly reports low-dimensional shapes in neural activations: circles for weekdays and months, helices for positions and counts, a paraboloid for colors, lines for temperature and age, polytopes for features in superposition. These shapes are usually identified and fitted by hand. We (i) compile a cited catalog of empirically reported neural geometries, (ii) train a permutation-invariant network \emph{only on synthetic shapes} that maps a 3D point cloud (the top-3 principal components of activations) to one of 13 shape families and to a canonical, unique parameter vector, using a shape-to-shape Chamfer loss against the ground-truth shape plus a point-to-shape residual, and (iii) evaluate it on a preregistered synthetic test set and on real activations of GPT-2, Llama-3.1-8B, a grokked modular-addition transformer and toy models of superposition, against a classical fit-every-family baseline with geometric-AIC model selection, a feature-based classifier, trivial baselines, random controls and ablations.

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A growing body of work shows that many concepts in language models are represented not by single directions but by low-dimensional manifolds \citep{engels2025notall,bhalla2026sae,gurnee2025linebreaks}. The common workflow is: collect activations for a set of inputs that vary along one concept, project onto the top principal components, look at the 2D/3D scatter, and describe the shape (``a circle'', ``a helix''), often followed by a hand-designed parametric fit \citep{kantamneni2025trig}. This is slow, subjective, and vulnerable to a known artifact: principal component analysis (PCA) of \emph{any} smoothly varying ordinal variable yields Fourier-like components and therefore an arch or a helix-like curve in the top components even when nothing ``curved'' is represented \citep{novembre2008horseshoe,diaconis2008horseshoes,antognini2018walks,shinn2023phantom}.

We ask whether the description step can be automated: given the top-3 PCA coordinates of a set of activations, predict which shape family it is and the parameters that uniquely specify the shape, such that the shape passes as close as possible to the points. Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A catalog of empirically reported neural geometries with model, layer, method, whether the shape is visible in the top-3 PCs, and verified sources (Sec.~\ref{sec:catalog}).
\item A 13-family shape vocabulary covering the catalog, with differentiable samplers, label margins between nested families, and canonical forms that make the output parameters unique (Sec.~\ref{sec:families}), including a family for the PCA ``ringing'' confounder.
\item A Set Transformer fitter trained only on on-the-fly synthetic shapes with domain randomization, a shape-to-shape Chamfer loss, and test-time refinement (Sec.~\ref{sec:method}).
\item A preregistered evaluation (hypotheses H1--H5) on synthetic and real activations with baselines, controls, ablations, bootstrap confidence intervals and multiple-testing correction (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}--\ref{sec:results}); both positive and negative results are reported.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and related work}
\label{sec:catalog}
\paragraph{Empirical neural geometries.} Circles for days of the week and months were found with sparse-autoencoder clusters and PCA in GPT-2, Mistral-7B and Llama-3-8B \citep{engels2025notall}; GPT-2's learned positional embeddings form a helix in their top-3 PCs \citep{yedidia2023helix}; the character count since the last line break lives on a rippled helix-like curve in Claude 3.5 Haiku \citep{gurnee2025linebreaks}; numbers form a generalized helix \citep{kantamneni2025trig}; Llama-3.1-8B layer 19 shows a paraboloid for colors, a helix for years, a circle for days and lines for temperature and age \citep{bhalla2026sae}; grokked modular-addition transformers place residues on circles (``clock'') or discs (``pizza'') \citep{nanda2023progress,zhong2023clock}; features in superposition form regular polytopes \citep{elhage2022tms}; true and false statements separate into clusters \citep{marks2024truth}; supervised MDS recovers circles, lines and clusters for temporal concepts \citep{tiblias2025smds}; in-context graphs reorganize into rings and grids \citep{park2025iclr}; emotions lie on a circle \citep{sun2026valence}. Outside LLMs, grid cells form a torus \citep{gardner2022torus}, head-direction cells a ring \citep{chaudhuri2019ring}, natural image patches a Klein bottle \citep{carlsson2008klein}. Appendix~\ref{app:catalog} (generated from \texttt{catalog.yaml}) lists all entries.

\paragraph{Primitive fitting.} Deep primitive fitting on CAD point clouds predicts per-point memberships and fits planes, spheres, cylinders and cones with differentiable least squares \citep{li2019spfn}, B-spline patches \citep{sharma2020parsenet} or regressed parameters \citep{yan2021hpnet}; the classical alternative is RANSAC \citep{schnabel2007ransac} with information-criterion model selection \citep{kanatani2004gaic}. These methods target large CAD clouds with several primitives; neural-geometry clouds have one shape, few points (7 weekdays), high noise, families absent from CAD vocabularies (helix, torus, PCA ringing), and come from a PCA projection of a much higher-dimensional space. To our knowledge, no prior work learns shape-type classification plus parameter regression for activation point clouds; the closest is hypothesis-driven supervised MDS, which needs concept labels and has no free shape parameters \citep{tiblias2025smds}.

\section{Shape vocabulary}
\label{sec:families}
Each family $f$ maps unconstrained raw parameters $\theta$ to a shape $S_f(\theta)\subset\mathbb{R}^3$, posed by a center $c$ and a rotation $R\in SO(3)$ predicted with the continuous 6D representation \citep{zhou2019rotation}. Table~\ref{tab:families} lists the families with their parameters. Each family provides (i) a differentiable dense sampler on a fixed grid with length/area weights, (ii) label margins so that a generated instance is not (nearly) a member of a simpler family at the sample's noise level (e.g.\ an arc's sagitta must exceed $\max(0.1\,\text{chord},3\sigma)$), and (iii) a canonicalization map that selects a unique representative among parameterizations of the same point set (axis signs fixed by the sign of the largest component, rotation about symmetry axes fixed by projecting the first PCA axis, point sets sorted lexicographically). The \emph{ringing} family $c+\sum_{k=1}^{3}a_k\cos(k\pi s)e_k$ is the shape PCA produces for smooth ordinal data \citep{novembre2008horseshoe,antognini2018walks}, so a helix claim must beat it.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\paragraph{Preprocessing.} Synthetic and real clouds pass through the same map: center, project onto the top-3 principal directions, fix axis signs by the sign of the third moment, force a right-handed frame (3D inputs), and divide by the RMS radius. No whitening is applied, so aspect ratios are preserved.

\paragraph{Synthetic data.} Each training step draws fresh shapes for all 13 families from a dedicated seed stream (test and validation streams are disjoint by construction). Nuisances implement domain randomization \citep{tobin2017domain}: $N\in[5,1024]$ log-uniform, relative noise $\sigma\in[0.003,0.15]$ log-uniform (10\% noise-free), anisotropic noise, non-uniform sampling densities, up to 10\% outliers, and, for 25\% of samples, embedding into $\mathbb{R}^D$ ($D\in\{16,64,256\}$) with noise in all $D$ dimensions followed by PCA back to 3D, which reproduces the real pipeline including projection effects and mirrored frames.

\paragraph{Model.} A Set Transformer encoder \citep{lee2019settransformer} (two ISAB blocks with 16 inducing points, PMA pooling, $d=128$, 4 heads, key-padding masks) maps per-point features (xyz and $\log N$) to an embedding; a classification head outputs logits over the 13 families and one MLP head per family outputs its raw parameters.

\paragraph{Loss.} For a sample of family $y$ with ground-truth shape $S^\ast$ and input points $X$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathrm{CE}(\ell,y)+\mathrm{CD}\big(S_y(\hat\theta_y),S^\ast\big)+0.5\,\mathrm{Res}\big(X\to S_y(\hat\theta_y)\big)+\lambda(t)\,\mathrm{Aux},
\end{equation}
where CD is the symmetric, length/area-weighted, unsquared Chamfer distance between dense samples of the predicted and of the noise-free ground-truth shape (restricted to its true extent), Res is a Huber point-to-shape residual, and Aux is an annealed axis/scale term $1-(\hat a\cdot a^\ast)^2+|\log\hat s-\log s^\ast|$. Because CD compares point sets it is invariant to every parameter symmetry, so no canonicalization or matching is needed during training. Only the ground-truth family's head receives geometric losses (teacher forcing, as in \citealp{li2019spfn}).

\paragraph{Inference.} The top-3 families are each refined for 300 Adam steps on the two-sided objective $\frac{1}{N}\sum_i d(x_i,S)+0.25\sum_j w_j d(s_j,X)$, initialized by the network (amortized inference followed by optimization, cf.\ \citealp{kolotouros2019spin}); the second term prevents degenerate inflation of radii and extents. The family is selected by the temperature-calibrated classifier \citep{guo2017calibration}; geometric AIC \citep{kanatani2004gaic} and a hybrid rule are ablations. The output is mapped back to PCA coordinates and canonicalized.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Preregistration.} Hypotheses, metrics, analysis plan and falsification criteria were committed before training (\texttt{reports/preregistration.md}); deviations are logged in \texttt{reports/deviations.md}.
\paragraph{Synthetic test set.} 250 samples per cell of 13 families $\times$ 4 point-count bins $\times$ 3 noise bins (39{,}000 clouds) from the \texttt{test} stream. Baselines run on a fixed subset of 20 samples per family and cell; all paired comparisons use that subset.
\paragraph{Baselines.} B1: classical multi-start fit of every family with the same two-sided objective (inits from the PCA frame and its axis permutations, k-means for clusters, every polytope subtype) and geometric-AIC selection. B2: hand-crafted features (global and local PCA eigenvalue ratios \citep{weinmann2015features}, persistent-homology H0/H1 lifetimes via ripser \citep{tralie2018ripser}, radial statistics) with gradient-boosted trees, then B1's fit of the predicted family. B3: always blob, always circle, random shape.
\paragraph{Ablations.} A1 parameter-space loss, A2 no residual term, A3 no refinement, A4 DeepSets encoder \citep{zaheer2017deepsets}, A5 selection rule, A6 no $\mathbb{R}^D$ augmentation; A1/A2/A4/A6 are compared with A0, the unchanged method trained with the same shorter budget.
\paragraph{Real activations.} R1 GPT-2 positional embeddings \citep{radford2019gpt2,yedidia2023helix}; R2 embeddings of a grokked 1-layer modular-addition transformer \citep{nanda2023progress}; R3 toy models of superposition with 3 hidden dimensions \citep{elhage2022tms}; R4 Llama-3.1-8B layer-5 mean activations per character count since the last newline \citep{gurnee2025linebreaks}; R5 weekday and month tokens in the templates of \citet{engels2025notall} (layer 5); R6--R8 years, colors, temperature and age with the templates of \citet{bhalla2026sae} (layer 19, last token); R9 true/false city statements \citep{marks2024truth} (layer 12). ``Layer $L$'' is the Hugging Face hidden state after $L$ blocks. Controls: C1 smooth Gaussian processes and random walks over an ordinal index in $\mathbb{R}^{768}$ (expected: ringing); C2 matched-covariance Gaussians for each dataset (expected: blob); C3 the same inputs through randomly initialized weights.
\paragraph{Statistics.} Percentile bootstrap CIs (2000 replicates); paired bootstrap and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm correction \citep{holm1979}; for real data, 200 random 80\% subsamples (PCA recomputed) give label frequencies, a 5-fold held-out point residual compares method, B1, helix and ringing fits, and exact binomial CIs plus a label-permutation null test agreement with the literature.
