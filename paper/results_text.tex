\paragraph{Where the gap comes from.} Selecting among the network's own refined candidates with geometric AIC (A5) lowers accuracy to \accAfivegaic{}, close to B2. Most of the gap to B1 is therefore model selection rather than fitting: with nested families and realistic noise a residual-versus-complexity criterion often prefers the more flexible family, while the classifier has learned the prior over families and noise that generated the data. A5 still chooses only among the classifier's top three candidates, so it is not a purely classical selector. Against B1 the accuracy gap grows with the number of points and is smallest for the smallest clouds; against B2 it is largest for the smallest clouds (Fig.~\ref{fig:byn}). The hardest families are cone, helix and paraboloid. The largest single confusions are helix$\leftrightarrow$cone, paraboloid$\leftrightarrow$cone and sphere$\leftrightarrow$cone, but most helix and paraboloid errors go to other families (Appendix, Fig.~\ref{fig:confusion}). Temperature scaling with $T=\temperature$ fitted on the validation stream lowers the expected calibration error from \eceBefore{} to \eceAfter{} and the NLL from \nllBefore{} to \nllAfter{}; the NLL-optimal temperature on the test set itself is \temperatureTest{} (Appendix, Fig.~\ref{fig:reliability}).

\subsection{Real activations}
Table~\ref{tab:real} lists every real dataset and control, and Fig.~\ref{fig:real} shows the clouds with the fitted shapes. As preregistered, H3 uses the majority coarse label over random 80\% subsamples. It agrees with the literature on \hthreeAgree{}/\hthreeN{} preregistered datasets; the label-permutation null gives $p=\hthreeP{}$, so \textbf{H3 is not supported} (it is not falsified either, since agreement is not below 50\%). Post hoc, the full-cloud label would agree on \hthreeAgreeFull{}/\hthreeN{}. The twelve datasets count the toy model R3 twice, with 6 and with 8 features; this split was fixed in the code before extraction and is logged as a deviation.

\paragraph{Successes.} The toy model of superposition with 6 features (R3) is labelled \fitRthreetmsnsix{} ($p=\pRthreetmsnsix{}$), with a fitted vertex on every feature direction: three orthogonal antipodal pairs (``digons'' in the terminology of \citealp{elhage2022tms}), i.e.\ an octahedron. Llama's character-count manifold (R4) is labelled \fitRfourllamacounting{} ($p=\pRfourllamacounting{}$), a curve as in \citet{gurnee2025linebreaks}; under the preregistered rule a helix claim is not made because ringing is not rejected. Temperature (R8a) is labelled \fitReightallamatemperature{}, the arch PCA produces when similarity decays smoothly with an ordinal variable \citep{novembre2008horseshoe,antognini2018walks}, which agrees with the ``line'' of \citet{bhalla2026sae} at the coarse level. Colours (R7) are labelled \fitRsevenllamacolors{}, a curved surface as reported (paraboloid in \citealp{bhalla2026sae}).

\paragraph{Misses and their likely causes (post-hoc inspection, not tested).} At the coarse level, H3 is missed on five datasets. (i) GPT-2's positional embeddings (R1) are a 1D spiral whose radius varies along the curve; no family represents it (helix has constant radius, ringing three harmonics), and the classifier selects \fitRonegpttwowpos{} ($p=\pRonegpttwowpos{}$), a surface the spiral lies on. The held-out residual cannot reveal this error because held-out points also lie on the sphere. (ii) Years (R6) keep only \varRsixllamayears{} of the variance in the top three components and are flagged ($\lambda_3/\lambda_4<1.5$): the 3D view cuts through a higher-dimensional structure. (iii) Age (R8b) is not one smooth curve: ordered by age, consecutive points jump apart around round decade values; it is labelled \fitReightbllamaage{}. (iv) True/false city statements (R9) form two overlapping clouds of very different spread, outside the vocabulary (labelled \fitRninellamatruthcities{}). (v) The 8-feature toy model (R3) is labelled \fitRthreetmsneight{} on the full cloud ($p=\pRthreetmsneight{}$), but most 80\% subsamples, which keep 6 of its 8 vertices, are labelled \majRthreetmsneight{} (\majfreqRthreetmsneight{}), so it counts as a miss under the preregistered rule. At the fine level, two more failures do not affect H3: weekdays and months (R5) form one tight cluster per token, 7 and 12 clusters, more than the 6 centroids the clusters family allows, and the fitter threads a \fitRfiveallamaweekdays{} through them; the grokked embeddings (R2) keep only \varRtwogrokkingWE{} of the variance in the top three components, as expected for the several Fourier circles of a grokked model \citep{nanda2023progress}, and are labelled \fitRtwogrokkingWE{} instead of a circle.

\paragraph{Controls (H4).} Matched-covariance Gaussians are labelled blob in \hfourBlob{} of draws (mean over the \hfourNreal{} real datasets, exploratory ones included), and the ordinal-process controls ringing in \hfourRing{} versus helix in \hfourHelix{}, so H4 passes as preregistered. A post-hoc split shows that this pass relies on the random walks (ringing in \hfourWalkRing{}): the smooth Gaussian-process controls alone are labelled helix in \hfourGpHelix{}. The classifier therefore confuses smooth ordinal processes with helices, the confound the preregistered helix rule guards against; for the fully evaluated Gaussian-process control the held-out residual favours ringing and the rule rejects the helix claim. Post hoc, the same rule was applied to every full-cloud helix fit with held-out folds: it rejects the helix claim for \helixRuleRejected{} but accepts it for \helixRuleAccepted{}, all of which the literature describes as circles. The rule separates helices from ringing, not from circles or from clusters threaded by a helix. Randomly initialised GPT-2 positional embeddings and grokking embeddings are labelled blob, but randomly initialised Llama is labelled \fitCthreellamamonthsinit{} for the month prompts and \fitCthreellamaweekdaysinit{} for the weekday prompts: Fig.~\ref{fig:controls} shows genuine clusters, one per input token, because a random transformer still separates token identities. The preregistered expectation ``blob'' for this control was wrong, not the fitter.
